---
title: Service registry
description: Payments go to registered services, named by id, never to a raw address.
---

The **service registry** is a separate contract, `service-registry`, that maps a service id such as `inference-credits` to the Stellar address that receives payment for it (the **payee**).

## Why payments take an id, not an address

The team account's `pay` function takes a service id. It has no parameter for a destination address. At the end of its checks the contract asks the registry to resolve the id, and pays whatever address the registry returns.

This removes a whole class of mistakes and attacks: a mistyped address, a pasted address from a phishing message, or an address invented by a language model cannot be expressed as a payment.

## What can happen to a service

| State | Effect on payment |
| --- | --- |
| Registered, active | Resolves to its payee. Payment can proceed. |
| Unknown id | Resolution fails and the payment reverts. |
| Suspended | Resolution fails and the payment reverts until the service is resumed. |

## Who controls what

- The **registry owner** registers services and can **suspend** or **resume** them. Service ids are never reused.
- A service's **payee** can **rotate its payout address**, for example to move to a new wallet.

Because the payee can change, an [approved exception](/concepts/approvals) records the payee at the time of the request, and settlement refuses if the payee has changed since. The owner then has to look at the request again.

## Services and spenders

A spender's policy lists the service ids it may pay. The contract checks the allowlist before it checks the registry, so a payment to a registered service that is not on a spender's list fails for that spender. A person can ask the owner for a one-off [approval](/concepts/approvals); an agent cannot.

See [Contracts reference](/contracts#service-registry) for the functions.
