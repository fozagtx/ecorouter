The app lives at [ecorouter.xyz/app](https://ecorouter.xyz/app/). It offers two ways in: **Sandbox**, which runs entirely in your browser with test dollars, and **Stellar testnet**, which uses the real contracts and a Freighter wallet.

<Callout type="warn">
Testnet only, not audited. Never send real funds to an EcoRouter contract.
</Callout>

## Part 1: the sandbox

The sandbox needs no wallet and no install. It runs the same rules as the contract, in the same order, using a TypeScript mirror that is tested case for case against the contract's tests. Nothing you do there touches a network, and it stays in your browser until you reset it.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the sandbox

Go to [/app/](https://ecorouter.xyz/app/) and choose **Open the sandbox**. You get a team account called Northwind Labs, funded with $5,000 in test dollars. You are **Ada**, the owner. The team also has **Marcus**, a person, and two AI agents, **Scout** and **Atlas**, each with its own policy. A payment from Marcus is already waiting for your decision.

</Step>
<Step>

### Read the rules

Open **Spenders**. Marcus has $1,000 a month, $250 per payment, 20 payments a month, and four services on his list: inference credits, web search, market data and `translate`. Nobody registered `translate`, so the registry will refuse it. Use **Add spender** to add someone of your own.

</Step>
<Step>

### Pay inside the rules

In the sidebar, switch **Signed in as** to Marcus. Open **New payment**, pick *Inference credits* and enter `120`. Before you submit, the right-hand panel runs every check in order with the arithmetic, for example `$180.00 + $120.00 = $300.00 of $1,000.00`. Pay, and a receipt appears in **Activity**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Go over a limit

Enter `420` instead. The cap is $250, so the panel shows **Over the per-payment cap, $170.00 over**, and the checks after it are not reached. Submit it anyway: the payment is **held**, and nothing moves. Choose **Ask the owner to approve it**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Approve, then settle

Switch back to Ada and open **Approvals**. Each request shows the spender, service, amount, the rule that caught it and how far over it was. **Approve** moves no money. **Settle payment** is a separate step that pays the service, as long as its payee has not changed since the request was filed.

</Step>
<Step>

### Try the assistant

As Marcus, open **Assistant** and try the examples. `Top up our inference credits by $120` passes all five stages. `Pay Acme Labs $50 for consulting` is refused by the schema, because the assistant has no way to name a service that is not on the list. `$35 of translation…` passes the schema but the registry refuses it. `Pay $420 for inference…` is held by the policy.

</Step>
<Step>

### Watch the agents

Open **Agents**. Scout buys $4 of web search every day; Atlas buys $12 of storage every week. Press **Run now**, or use **Settings → +1 day** to move the sandbox clock forward. One of Atlas's earlier payments was over its cap: it was refused and stopped there, because agents cannot ask for approval.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Part 2: Stellar testnet with Freighter

On testnet the money is test USDC held by the `team-account` contract, services resolve through the `service-registry` contract, and every payment links to a transaction on the Stellar explorer.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Deploy the contracts

You need the [Stellar CLI](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tools/cli) and Rust with the `wasm32v1-none` target. From the repository root:

```bash
./scripts/deploy-testnet.sh
```

The script creates and funds an `owner` identity, deploys the registry and a team account owned by it, registers five demo services, and writes the contract ids to `packages/app/.env.local`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Fund the account

Get testnet USDC (for example from Circle's testnet faucet) and send it to the team-account contract address. There is no deposit function; the contract simply holds what is sent to it, and only the owner can withdraw.

</Step>
<Step>

### Connect Freighter

Install Freighter, switch it to **Testnet**, and import the owner key (or propose a new owner with `propose_owner`). Run `pnpm dev:app`, choose **Connect Freighter**, and approve the connection. Every action is a transaction you sign in Freighter.

</Step>
<Step>

### Add spenders and pay

Add people by their own `G…` address; each signs their own payments from their own Freighter. For an agent, generate a key, add its address as an *AI agent* spender, and run the worker where the agent lives:

```bash
AGENT_SECRET=S... TEAM_ACCOUNT_ID=C... \
  pnpm --filter @ecorouter/app agent --service search --amount 4 --every day
```

The agent's account needs a little XLM for fees.

</Step>
</Steps>
