01 · How it works

One account, a rule for every spender

The owner funds one team account in USDC. The money stays in a Soroban contract, and every payment out of it is checked against the spender's own policy before anything settles.

  1. 01 Policy

    An allowance for every spender

    Add a person or an AI agent and give it its own policy: an allowance per period, a cap per payment, a maximum number of payments, an expiry date and the services it may pay. Pause or remove a spender at any time.

    allowance $900 / month
    cap per payment $250
    payments 20 / month
    expires Dec 31
  2. 02 Registry

    Only registered services get paid

    Payments go to services in an on-chain registry, never to a pasted address. The pay function takes a service id. An id that is unknown or suspended reverts, so nobody can redirect money to an address they typed.

    pay(…, service) "inference-credits"
    resolves to G…Q7XK (payee)
    unknown id reverts
    suspended reverts
  3. 03 Approval

    Over the limit, the owner decides

    A payment over a rule is held, not failed. Nothing moves. The spender asks for approval, and the owner sees the rule that caught it and how far over it was. Approving and paying are separate steps.

    status held
    rule cap $250
    over by $70
    owner approve · decline
02 · Agents and people

Your agents spend like teammates, under tighter rules

An AI agent is just another spender on the account. It gets its own Stellar key and its own policy, and it can never sign for more than that policy allows.

Agents Its own key, its own policy

Pay on a schedule, top up a service, stop at the line

  • Scheduled payments. Pay $5 every day to a data API, or top up inference credits when they run low.
  • Separate keys. Each agent signs with its own Stellar key. Removing the agent removes its ability to pay.
  • Refused means refused. An agent's payment that breaks a rule stops there. It does not turn into an approval request.
  • An assistant that cannot invent a payee. Its output has no address field, so it can only choose from services already on the spender's list.

People

People pay from the same account

Teammates pay approved services from the app with an amount and a memo. The app pre-checks the policy and shows the arithmetic before anything is signed.

Control

The owner keeps the last word

Raise a cap, pause a spender, remove a service from a list, or approve one exception. Requests over a limit land on the owner's Approvals screen.

Receipts

Every payment leaves a receipt

Settled, held, declined or refused, each attempt is recorded with its spender, service, amount, memo and rule, and settled ones link to the Stellar transaction.

Stellar

Settled in USDC on Stellar

The money sits in a Soroban contract, not in anyone's wallet. Payments settle in seconds for a fraction of a cent, so small daily payments make sense.

    04 · Security

    The agent proposes. Only the contract authorizes.

    You can ask the assistant to pay in plain words. What it produces is a proposal that has to survive five stages, and any one of them can refuse. The last one is the contract, and it does not trust anything before it.

    1. 01 Sentence
      "top up our inference credits by $120"

      Code, not the model, reads the amount. No amount, or two different amounts, and it stops here.

      Refuses: no clear amount
    2. 02 Assistant
      service: inference-credits

      The assistant may only choose a service from the spender's approved list, or say none fits.

      Refuses: no matching service
    3. 03 Schema
      { service, amount, memo }

      The output must match a fixed shape. There is no address field, so an invented payee cannot be expressed.

      Refuses: wrong shape
    4. 04 Registry
      inference-credits → payee

      The service id must resolve in the on-chain registry and must not be suspended.

      Refuses: unknown or suspended
    5. 05 Policy
      $120 ≤ cap $250 · ≤ $560 left

      The contract runs the spender's policy. In policy it pays; over a limit it is held.

      Refuses: over a rule
    Inside the contract

    Every call to pay() runs the same checks, in the same order

    The app and the assistant pre-check so you see problems early, but they are a convenience. The rules that matter are in the team account contract, and a failing check means no transfer happens.

    Honest status

    Stellar testnet only. Not audited. Use test funds. The sandbox runs a TypeScript mirror of the same rules in your browser for demos.

    05 · Why Stellar

    Rules written into the account, settled in seconds

    A shared card or a shared key trusts whoever holds it. On Stellar the spending rules live in the team account itself, so every payment is checked on chain, by the contract that holds the money, before it settles.

    01

    The money sits in a contract

    Team funds are held by a Soroban contract, not by any one person's wallet or by an agent's key. A leaked agent key can only do what that agent's policy allows.

    02

    USDC is native on Stellar

    Payments are in USDC through the Stellar Asset Contract, kept in exact 7-decimal integer units, so the arithmetic you see is the arithmetic that runs.

    03

    Small payments make sense

    Ledgers close in seconds and fees are a fraction of a cent, so an agent paying $5 a day to a data service costs what it should.

    One policy per spender
    • allowance How much per period
    • max_payment The most any one payment can be
    • max_payments How many payments per period
    • period Length of one period, in seconds
    • expires_at After this, the policy stops authorizing
    • services The only service ids this spender may pay

    06 · FAQ

    Questions

    What is EcoRouter?

    A shared USDC spending account on Stellar for a team and its AI agents. The owner funds one account, and every person or agent pays from it under its own policy: an allowance per period, a cap per payment, a maximum number of payments, an expiry date and a list of approved services. A Soroban contract holds the money and enforces the policy.

    Can I use real money?

    Not yet. EcoRouter runs on Stellar testnet only and the contracts have not been audited. Use test funds. You can also try everything in the sandbox, which runs the same rules in your browser with test dollars.

    Can an assistant spend without limits?

    No. The assistant only proposes a payment. Code reads the amount from your sentence, the assistant can only pick a service from the spender's approved list, its output has no address field, and the contract then runs the full policy. Any of those stages can refuse, and the contract has the final say.

    What happens when a payment is blocked?

    If it breaks a limit such as the cap or the allowance, it is held, not failed, and nothing moves. A person can ask for approval, and the owner sees the rule that caught it and how far over it was. Approving and paying are separate steps, and the payment refuses to settle if the service's payee changed in between. Payments from a paused spender, or to an unknown or suspended service, are refused outright.

    Do I need a wallet?

    Not for the sandbox, which needs nothing installed. To use Stellar testnet you connect a Freighter wallet, and the team account and service registry contracts run on testnet.

    Can my team add an AI agent?

    Yes. Each agent is a spender with its own Stellar key and its own policy. It can pay on a schedule, for example $5 every day to a data API, or top up a service. When an agent's payment breaks a rule it stops there and does not become an approval request.

    Give your team and its agents a budget they cannot break

    Open the sandbox and run a team with test dollars in your browser, no wallet needed. When you are ready, connect Freighter and do the same on Stellar testnet.
